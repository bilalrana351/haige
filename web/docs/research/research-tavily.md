| Site | Register | Hero shape | Primary CTA | Dominant motif |

**Recurring conventions:** (1) name the engines explicitly in the hero — every single site does; (2) a "trusted by N" number within one scroll; (3) a category acronym (AEO/GEO/AXP); (4) dual CTA free-trial + demo; (5) **light mode dominates** — Profound is the only meaningfully dark one. A dark, restrained HAIGE would differentiate, but light-with-one-accent is the category default and lower-risk.

**Gap HAIGE can own:** nobody in this set leads with *approval / human supervision / evidence*. Every one of them leads with detection ("see where you show up") or aspiration ("be the answer"). The closest is Rankscale's "not just a score". Positioning HAIGE as "it finds the drop, shows the evidence, drafts the fix, and waits for you" is genuinely unoccupied in this category.

## Q2. Copywriting conventions — verbatim examples

### Human-in-the-loop / approval (strongest verbatim set, mostly from AI-agent adjacents since GEO vendors don't use this language)

From **AiMarketer** (https://aimarketer.com) — the most complete HITL copy system found:
1. "Marketing on autopilot; **you just approve it**." (hero subhead)
2. "**You approve, it executes** — Nothing publishes without your sign-off. Guardrails and approvals are built for teams and brands that care."
3. "Launches, not just drafts — Copy generators stop at a draft. AiMarketer connects your channels and ships the ads, **with your approval**."
4. "Connect — Plug in your site and channels… **Read-only until you say go**." ← excellent, concrete, low-hype. Steal the *mechanism*, not the words.
5. "Create — Ads, posts, emails and landing copy, generated on-brand and **ready for your review**."
6. "Launch — **You approve once.** The agent schedules and publishes across every connected channel."
7. "**Approval workflow.** Nothing goes live without sign-off from you or a role you delegate."
8. "**Brand-safety guardrails.** Tone, claims and topics the agent must respect, enforced on every asset."
9. "It never sleeps, never forgets the brand guidelines, and **never publishes anything you have not signed off**."
10. "Your data stays yours. We never train public models on your account. Ever."

From **Moonrank** (https://www.moonrank.ai):
11. "One LinkedIn post a day, drafted from the content you already publish. Turn your articles, positioning and brand voice into a daily LinkedIn draft — **ready for you to review, edit and publish in a click**."

From category/analyst writing (useful phrasing, not site copy):
12. "**Autonomy on reads, human-in-the-loop on writes.**" — buildmvpfast.com. This is the sharpest one-line articulation of HAIGE's model I found.
13. "The agent proposes, a human authorizes before execution." — Omdia (omdia.tech.informa.com), which found 27% of enterprises use exactly this "Approval" mode vs 25% "Oversight" and 23% "Collaboration". Useful stat for a credibility line.
14. "The owner sees **the proposed action, the reason, the source evidence, the dollar exposure, and the expected outcome**." — getclaw.sh. This is effectively a spec for HAIGE's approval card UI.
15. "A human builds the list and approves the copy. AI drafts variants… **The seller stays in the loop by design.**" — Overloop.
16. "An AI agent should ask for human approval before actions that affect customers, money, production systems, legal commitments, **brand reputation**, or irreversible business decisions." — win.sh.

### "Evidence" phrasing
- Rankscale: "prioritized next steps based on **where you are losing visibility, not just a score**"
- Otterly: "Know **why** AI skips your content — and fix it."
- Scrunch: "observe what agents say and do, **understand** and act"
- Semrush vocabulary: "cited pages", "mentions", "citation source analysis"
- AthenaHQ: "Citation source analysis and link building"
- Pattern: evidence is expressed as **citation provenance** ("which page got cited, from which prompt, on which engine"). HAIGE should use the noun *citation* and the phrase *the prompt that triggered it*.

### "Measure" phrasing
- Profound: "Understand, analyze, build, and **measure**"
- AirOps: "Know where to act, execute at a pace no one else can match, and **compound your results over time**"
- Rankscale: "Connect GA4 and Search Console to see how AI visibility **translates into real traffic**"
- AirOps proof: "3x increase in AI search citations — went from being recommended in 24 to 68 priority questions" ← measurement expressed as a *count of prompts you win*, which is far more credible than a composite "visibility score".

### "Across ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude" phrasing
- Gauge: "Reach the millions of users exploring ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude Code, and more."
- Otterly: "across ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Claude, Perplexity, Google AI Mode, Gemini, and Copilot"
- Rankscale: "across 17+ engines, including ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews"
- Ahrefs: "across AI answers, YouTube, and Reddit"
- Profound: engine name animates inside the H1.
- Convention: **list 4-6 named engines, then "and more"**. Don't say "LLMs" or "answer engines" in the hero without naming at least ChatGPT.

### Credible vs hype (judgement)
**Credible:** Rankscale's "(beta)" labels and "not just a score"; AiMarketer's "Read-only until you say go"; AirOps' specific, oddly-shaped metrics ("6%", "24 to 68 prompts"); Ahrefs' attributed founder quote; Otterly's before-quote/after-quote pair; Scrunch's "See results in 30 seconds / No credit card required".
**Hype / avoid:** "Own Your AI Presence", "Become the Brand AI Trusts", "Be the Answer for AI" — pure aspiration, interchangeable between all three, and unfalsifiable. "Marketing on autopilot" directly contradicts an approval promise — **HAIGE must never use "autopilot", "set and forget", "hands-free" or "AI does it all"**; they undercut the human-supervision differentiator. Also avoid unsourced round numbers ("Over 100 million people search with AI every day") without a link — pre-launch you have no authority to assert them.
**Rule of thumb for HAIGE:** every claim on the page should be either (a) a description of a mechanism you actually built, or (b) a number with a source link. Nothing else.

## Q3. Trust devices for pre-launch / early-stage B2B AI sites

**Stage-appropriate pattern** (hedrick.io, landing page patterns by startup stage):
| Stage | Primary goal | Key section | Social proof type |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-launch** | Email capture | Hero + waitlist form | **Founder credibility** |
| MVP/beta | Trial signups | Hero + product demo | Early user quotes |
| Growth | Demo bookings | Hero + … | Logos/case studies |

That table is the single most load-bearing finding for HAIGE: at pre-launch, **the founder is the social proof**. Don't fake the growth-stage furniture.

### Devices observed / recommended
1. **Founder note, attributed, first person.** Ahrefs runs it as a site-wide banner: "'The AI marketing platform we needed didn't exist. So we built it.' — Tim Soulo, CMO at Ahrefs". For HAIGE: a short signed note explaining why the approval gate exists, with a real name, role and photo, linking to LinkedIn/X. Costs nothing, is unfakeable, and is the only proof you legitimately have.
2. **Numbered "how it works" step diagram.** AiMarketer: "One loop, five steps, every week" — Connect → Plan → Create → Launch → Optimize, each with one sentence and a big numeral. Profound renders its steps as an actual workflow canvas with Output chips. For HAIGE the loop is: **Monitor → Diagnose (with evidence) → Draft → You approve → Publish → Measure** — and drawing it as a *loop* rather than a line is on-brand because the product is a loop.
3. **Explicit governance/security section.** Fellow (fellow.app) is the best template: headline "**Compliant by default. Controlled by you.**", supported by a bullet set — "Set the rules for recording", "Stay in control of the conversation", "Only keep meeting data for as long as you need it", "Govern access across the organization", "Your meeting data stays yours"; badges line "Our AI is never trained on your data · SOC 2 Type II, GDPR & HIPAA"; CTA "**Visit the Trust Center**". Pre-launch HAIGE can't claim SOC 2, so state what is true and dated: what you store, what you don't, who can approve, whether data trains models, and a "we're pursuing SOC 2" line with a date if honest.
4. **"What we don't do" honesty section.** Rare in the wild but the closest live analogues are Rankscale's "(beta)" labels and "No Upsell / no per-engine upsell tiers", and AiMarketer's "Read-only until you say go". Recommended for HAIGE as a short two-column block: *What HAIGE does / What HAIGE will not do* — e.g. "will not post anything without a named human approving it", "will not invent citations", "will not claim credit for traffic it can't trace". This is the rare pre-launch device that *gains* credibility from having no customers.
5. **Waitlist form.** Keep it to email + one qualifying field (company domain), state what happens next and when, and cap it with a reassurance line. Anti-patterns called out (getlaunchlist.com, beyondlabs.io): fake milestone counters ("we just hit X users"), countdowns, and long scroll-before-the-form. Put the form in the hero and repeat it once before the footer. Genesys Growth flags "excessive form fields creating friction" as a top-5 B2B landing error.
6. **Public changelog / build-in-public.** Peec ships "Changelog" as a top-level footer item alongside Docs and Reviews. For a pre-launch product a dated changelog (or "what shipped this week") is the cheapest proof that the thing is real and moving.
7. **Two-layer trust signals** (lovable.dev/blog): *credibility-layer* signals (logos, named customers, usage stats) belong **early on each page**; *submission-layer* signals (SSL, privacy statement, security note) belong **immediately adjacent to the form fields**, not in the footer.
8. **Real interface beats glossy concept** (flow-agency.com): "Real product visuals outperform glossy concepts every time. You can stylize it, crop, blur, highlight, but users need to see the actual interface." With no screenshots, the honest substitute is a **designed, non-photographic representation of real artifacts** — an approval card, a citation card, a diff, a Slack message block — rendered in your own design system and clearly stylised rather than a fake screenshot. Otterly's use of Adidas/Nike as visibly-demo data is the other legitimate route.
9. **Analyst/press badges** if any exist (Otterly: "AI in Marketing Cool Vendor by [Gartner]"; Athena: Forbes / WSJ / YC). Don't fabricate; a single "Built by [credential]" line is better than an empty logo row.

### Testimonial attribution formatting observed
- AirOps: `"quote" — George Bonaci, VP of Growth, Ramp` (full name, title, company; portrait avatar).
- Geekbot: `"quote" — Chris Brisson, CEO at CallLoop.com & SalesMessage.com` (photo avatar, lowercase domain in the company slot).
- Otterly: `"quote" — Rebecca Anderson, Content Marketing Manager at Instant Commerce`.
- Gauge Wall of Love: quote + **name + avatar only, no title, no company** on many entries ("I would die for Gauge" — Andrew Caslow). Short, hyperbolic, unattributed-by-role quotes read as *screenshotted Slack praise* — charming at Gauge's stage, but they are the exact pattern that looks fabricated when you have no brand equity. Avoid for HAIGE.

## Q4. Testimonial section design, 2025–2026

**Quantity.** Three to five testimonials is the sweet spot on a homepage — "fewer can seem sparse, while more can overwhelm visitors before they've explored" (provesrc.com, 75 Social Proof Statistics for 2026). Same source: homepage testimonials associated with up to ~34% conversion lift; testimonials near the fold outperform testimonials buried at the bottom. Treat the 34% as directional marketing-survey data, not a law.

**Layout.**
- **Single quote** works when it is specific and from a recognisable source: "A single testimonial quote can be powerful if it is specific and comes from a recognisable source: a named individual at a named company, describing a specific outcome. **Generic testimonials without attribution add almost nothing.** Place testimonials adjacent to the CTA rather than below it: social proof works hardest at the decision point." (alfdesigngroup.com)
- **3-card grid** is the default for a homepage with 3–5 quotes; keeps each quote readable and gives room for avatar + name + role + company.
- **Marquee / wall of love** (Gauge, Otterly's 40,000+ framing) signals volume, not substance. It only works when you genuinely have volume; at pre-launch it reads as padding. Also: an auto-scrolling marquee makes quotes unreadable and is a motion-accessibility problem.
- **Restraint near the decision point:** "Use the same typography, color logic, spacing, and image treatment as the page around it. Restrained layouts near decision points make the message easier to find." (embedsocial.com)

**Length.** One to three sentences. The credible ones in this research are hedged and specific ("The quality of our content since using AirOps has honestly increased pretty significantly"); the incredible ones are short superlatives ("I would die for Gauge").

**Attribution — required fields.** Full name, role, company, and either a real photo or the company logo. "The more information you collect, the more genuine your testimonials will be… If your customers do not want to share their names and photos for privacy reasons, you may use their initials" (redbaton.digital). Initials-in-a-circle is an acceptable fallback; a **generic stock-photo avatar is worse than no avatar** and is the top fake-tell.

**Avoiding fake-looking patterns:**
- Never invent quotes or use placeholder names — "fake testimonials are not recommended if you don't want to cause trouble" (mockplus.com); "Never use fake praise" (embedsocial.com).
- Tells that read as fake: stock-photo faces; five identical-length quotes; all quotes ending in an exclamation mark; no company named; no role; star ratings with no review source; suspiciously round metrics.
- Link out where possible (G2/LinkedIn/case study) — third-party verifiability is what separates a quote from a claim.
- **For HAIGE specifically (pre-launch, zero customers):** do not ship a testimonial section. Ship the *slot* in the design system, and fill the space with an advisor/design-partner block ("Design partners" with real logos if you have consent), the founder note, or a "From this / To this" problem-statement pair in Otterly's style where the "from" quote is a *paraphrased user-research finding, labelled as such*. Layer types of proof rather than faking one (userpilot.com).

## Q5. Footer, navigation and CTA conventions on premium SaaS marketing sites

### Navigation
- **~5 top-level items** is the benchmark: "A well-structured SaaS navigation has about 5 top-level items" (nerdcow.co.uk). Mega-menus expand from generic to specific; icons inside complex dropdowns make them scannable; "Don't rely on the primary CTA being just in your navigation — it needs [to be repeated in the page]".
- Red Hat design system caps secondary nav at a small fixed number of slots and warns against exceeding them.
- Orbit Media's SEO argument against bloated nav: every homepage link divides the authority passed to each destination — relevant to HAIGE given it sells discoverability.
- Observed counts: Gauge 9 (too many), Scrunch 5 groups, Rankscale ~4, Peec 4 (Product, Pricing, Resources, Partnerships), Profound ~8 mega-menus. **Peec's four is the right model for HAIGE** — e.g. Product / How it works / Pricing / Blog, with Log in + primary CTA on the right.

### Sticky nav
NN/g's "Sticky Headers: 5 Ways to Make Them Better" (nngroup.com) — persistent headers are useful when "unobtrusive, high-contrast, minimally animated, and fit user needs":
1. Maximize content-to-chrome ratio — keep the bar small (rule of thumb elsewhere: under ~10% of viewport height).
2. Contrast with the content beneath it matters — a transparent bar over scrolling content fails.
3. Keep motion minimal, natural, responsive.
4. Consider **partially persistent** headers (hide on scroll down, reappear on scroll up) — the best default for a long marketing page.
5. Consider whether you need one at all.
Plus: "The best sticky headers do not animate at all while the user is actively scrolling… When a shrinking transition is necessary, make that change **instantaneous rather than slowly morphing**" (cpcloudhosting.com). NN/g also notes sticky headers increase discoverability of nav and utility elements.
**Recommendation for HAIGE:** partially-persistent header, 56–64px, solid background (no blur-only), no shrink animation, primary CTA always present in it.

### Buttons
- "Perfectly square corners feel formal, precise, and corporate. Fully rounded 'pill' buttons feel friendly, modern, and approachable. **Slightly rounded rectangles (4–8px border-radius) strike a balance used by most major brands.**" (blakfy.com)
- Pills read "more friendly / less corporate"; sharp corners read serious, typical of financial/medical UI (r/Design; Cloud Four, "Jagged Little Pill").
- Cloud Four's practical caution: pill radii collapse to circles in tight space and interact badly with focus rings and multi-line labels.
- **Recommendation for HAIGE:** 8px radius (or 10px) on buttons and cards, consistent across the system. The product is a governance/approval tool for people who answer for the results — "friendly" pills undercut that. Reserve full-round only for small chips/avatars.
- **Primary + secondary pattern:** one solid primary (high contrast, single accent colour) + one ghost/outline secondary, side by side, primary first on LTR. Every site in Q1 uses exactly two. Common pairs: trial+demo (Scrunch, Otterly, AirOps), demo+login (Athena, Profound). For pre-launch HAIGE: **primary "Join the waitlist" / secondary "See how it works"** (anchor to the loop diagram) — a secondary that costs nothing and doesn't compete.
- Put a micro-reassurance line under the primary CTA: Scrunch's "See results in 30 seconds · No credit card required", Gauge's "Start for free", AiMarketer's "Early access, join the first wave. No card required. Your data stays yours."

### Footer
- **Don't duplicate the main nav.** B2B footer guidance (khod.io): scannable column headings — Product, Solutions, Resources, Company, Legal; short link labels; a conversion cue near the footer (demo button or small form) paired with credibility signals (logos, security badges, ratings).
- Minimal sites do well with a **utility-only footer**: "essential company links… user safety rails and compliance essentials" (eleken.co, Footer UX in 2026). Same source: footers are no longer mandatory on every page; heavy sticky mobile footers eat screen space; show "last updated" dates on policy links.
- For a small site: three to five nav links, contact, social, newsletter (depechecode.io).
- Pre-footer CTA block is standard practice — Grammarly's is cited as the model: lead-gen CTA + email signup, adjacent to a proactive **AI-and-security trust block** (soliddigital.com). That adjacency (CTA next to "here's how we handle your data and your brand voice") is exactly right for HAIGE.
- **Recommended HAIGE footer:** pre-footer CTA band (waitlist form + one-line reassurance) → 3 columns (Product / Company / Legal) + wordmark + one status-ish line (changelog link, "last updated" on policies) → copyright. No badge soup.

## Q6. Slack + web-app dual-surface products — communicating "lives in Slack" without screenshots

### What the real ones do
**Geekbot** (https://geekbot.com) — the most Slack-native positioning found:
- Hero: "Work better together wherever you are!" / "The easiest way to run Standups, Polls and Surveys **in Slack and Microsoft Teams**."
- **CTAs are the install actions themselves: "Add to Slack" and "Add to Teams"** — the Slack green/brand button *is* the primary CTA. Strongest possible signal of surface, zero screenshots needed.
- Under-CTA reassurance: "Free for teams of up to 10 users - no credit card required" + Capterra 4.9 rating badge.
- Copy carries the surface, not imagery: "**The bot comes to you!** Geekbot kicks off a Standup with your team, they respond, and updates are shared with zero effort."; "…right inside Slack and Teams."; "Scheduled summaries that highlight your team's participation, blockers, and sentiment."
- Visuals are abstract shapes ("decorative half sphere", "decorative background shapes") plus avatar testimonials — not product screenshots.

**Linear** (https://linear.app/integrations/slack) — an integration page, not the homepage, and it is almost entirely prose:
- Title: "**Create issues from Slack messages and sync threads**"
- Structured as Overview → **How it works** → Configure → Setup → Commonly used tools.
- Mechanism described in verbs, not pictures: "Create Linear issues from Slack… by mentioning @Linear"; "the comment thread in Slack will be **bidirectionally synced** with a thread in the issue"; "Links from Linear sent in Slack will **expand to show a preview** with key properties… Expanded links include actions so that members can update issues directly from Slack"; notification routing to "dedicated Slack channels to keep everyone in the company updated **regardless of whether they have Linear accounts**".
- That last clause is the reusable idea: **Slack is where the people who don't log in get reached.** For HAIGE: approvers (a CMO, a founder) get the approval request in Slack and never have to open the web app; the web app is where the evidence and history live.

**Loom** (Slack Marketplace listing): "Enhance your Slack conversations with context-rich video messages. Need to add clarity, context, or nuance to a Slack thread while avoiding yet another meeting?" — framed as *the Slack problem it removes*, not as a feature.

**Lovable** (blog, Salesforce announcement): "…run **wherever your team already works**: Headless 360, Slack, and installable agents." — the canonical phrase for this concept is "**where your team already works**".

### Techniques for showing Slack without a fake screenshot
1. **Make the install the CTA.** An "Add to Slack" button (using Slack's official button from the Slack Media Kit, slack.com/media-kit — logos, colour, typography and usage guidelines are published) communicates the surface instantly and is legitimate pre-launch if it leads to a waitlist explaining Slack is coming — label it honestly if it isn't live.
2. **Render the message, not the client.** Design a single Slack-*shaped* message block in your own design system — avatar square with rounded corners, bold bot name + "APP" tag, message text, a divider, and two action buttons ("Approve", "Request changes") — floating on your own background, clearly stylised, no Slack chrome (no sidebar, no channel list, no window frame). It reads as "this is what arrives" rather than "here is a screenshot of our product".
3. **Use Block Kit vocabulary as the design language.** Slack's Block Kit components (section, actions, context, buttons, modals) are a published, documented visual grammar; a HAIGE approval card built from those primitives is both accurate and buildable later. Slack's Block Kit Builder can generate the real thing when you do build it.
4. **Two-surface diagram.** A simple split: left = Slack (where alerts, drafts and the Approve/Reject action happen), right = web app (where evidence, history, and measurement live), with a labelled arrow loop between. Linear's Slack page is effectively this idea in prose; drawing it is clearer and avoids fake UI entirely.
5. **Carry it in copy.** Lines that do the work without an image: "Approvals arrive in Slack. The evidence lives in the app." / "Your approvers never have to log in." / "The bot comes to you." (Geekbot) / "wherever your team already works" (Lovable).
6. **Use the official Slack mark, correctly.** Slack Media Kit (https://slack.com/media-kit) publishes logos, colour, typography, writing and illustration guidelines — using the real mark in a "Works with Slack"-style lockup is a trust device; a redrawn or recoloured Slack logo is the opposite.

## Consolidated recommendations for the HAIGE landing page

1. **Hero:** state the mechanism, name the engines. Something in the shape of "Find out why you disappeared from ChatGPT — and fix it, with your approval." Subhead names Google, ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, LinkedIn, X, Reddit.
2. **Lead with the gap nobody occupies:** evidence + human approval. Never use "autopilot".
3. **Three-up spec strip** under the hero (Rankscale pattern): Monitors everywhere / Diagnoses with evidence / Nothing publishes without you.
4. **The loop diagram** as the centrepiece instead of screenshots: Monitor → Diagnose → Draft → Approve → Publish → Measure, drawn as a cycle, with the approval gate visually emphasised.
5. **Slack:** one stylised approval-message card + the line "Approvals arrive in Slack. The evidence lives in the app."
6. **Trust block:** signed founder note + a "What HAIGE won't do" two-column honesty section + a plain data/governance paragraph. No testimonials, no fake logos, no invented metrics.
7. **Chrome:** 4 nav items, partially-persistent 56–64px header, 8px radius, one solid primary + one ghost secondary, micro-reassurance under the CTA, pre-footer waitlist band, three-column utility footer with a changelog link.
